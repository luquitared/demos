Motivation

Large-scale egocentric human-demonstration corpus for robot-manipulation foundation-model training: continuous, multi-view first-person work sessions with synchronized wrist and exocentric views, per-frame hand tracking, and dense subtask language labels. Collected and produced by Synjuku.

By category: home 53% · office & conference 18% · hospitality 16% · retail 13%. Every environment type is captured at up to 9 different physical venues, and every episode is a single continuous session — no cuts, no concatenation.

Collection

Worker-worn 4-camera rigs operated by consented collectors performing task-card-scripted activities in real working venues. Each of the 232 tasks is defined by a versioned task card (objects, initial/final state, step list), giving controlled coverage of manipulation skills across environments. Multi-camera synchronization via audio-correlation alignment at capture processing time.

Quality control

  • Delivery gates (per episode): camera coverage across all wearable angles, cross-camera synchronization, minimum duration, file-integrity (SHA-256 verified at upload, storage, and delivery), black/corrupt-stream rejection. Episodes failing any gate were rejected before delivery.
  • Hand tracking: per-frame, with explicit NaN marking when hands leave frame — no interpolated or fabricated values.
  • Language labels are automatically generated, video-grounded subtask annotations produced alongside collection and spot-audited. A per-span verified-label edition of any subset is available on request (see the GDM pilot sample for the verified-label methodology and its measured results).

Access & use

Read-only scoped S3 credentials to s3://synjuku-data/synjuku-D1-v3/. Intended uses: manipulation policy and representation learning at scale, hand-trajectory prediction, long-horizon subtask segmentation, multi-view learning. Continuous ~44-minute sessions make this corpus suited to long-context training in addition to clip-level use; windowed exports (e.g. 60 s clips) can be produced on request. Maintained by the Synjuku data team.
