Kalmari — Go-to-Market Plan

AI-audited Kalshi picks with a public track record, copy-tradeable in one tap from Telegram · prepared June 9, 2026 · repo tg-research/frontrun-bot

Companion documents: Competitive Landscape Research (June 3) · Product & Code Audit (June 9, internal — not published)

The strategy in one paragraph. Kalmari is not a trading bot; it is a publicly accountable research desk that happens to execute. Every competitor in the Telegram prediction-market space competes on execution convenience or social/copy features; none publishes AI-generated picks against a verifiable track record. That track record — the /performance page and leaderboard already built — is the entire marketing engine. The plan: harden the launch blockers, reprice around signal access (not LLM tokens), seed 50–100 real users from Kalshi-native communities, and let the product's own share pages, settled-pick results, and weekly recap content do the distribution. Paid acquisition waits until the track record has 3+ months of public history and the funnel converts.

1What you're selling

The product as it exists today (post-0af6340, signals-first):

  • Two research feeds. AI mispricing audits across thousands of Kalshi markets, plus podcast-claim signals (speaker predictions mapped to live markets). Delivered in Telegram via /signals.
  • One-tap copy trading. Any pick → live-repriced proposal → confirm. Drift detection warns when the edge has been eaten.
  • Paper mode by default. $100 simulated, filled at live prices, zero risk. Live mode is opt-in via the user's own Kalshi API key (non-custodial, BYOK, encrypted at rest).
  • A public, verifiable track record. Leaderboard by model/speaker/topic, the /performance explorer with per-pick theses and outcomes, shareable signal pages (/s/<id>) and portfolio pages (/p/<runID>).
  • Retention machinery already built: weekly digest, deep links (/start sig_<id>), settlement tracking, insider-tier auto-copy.

What the June 3 competitive research established: one-click Telegram execution is commoditized (pvp.trade, okbet, FrenFlow, TradePolyBot all do it); the crowded lane is social/copy trading; and nobody ships AI research picks or podcast→market matching in a consumer Telegram product. The only AI-research comparable (OctagonAI) is a developer CLI. Kalshi-native, research-led, consumer UX is open lane.

The honest constraint: the AI-research half is imitable — any competitor can bolt on an LLM. The two things that compound and can't be retroactively faked are (a) the public track record's history, and (b) the podcast→market pipeline. Both should be over-weighted in everything below.

2Positioning & messaging

Category and frame

Do not enter the "Telegram trading bot" category — it's owned by execution/social players and carries memecoin-bot trust baggage. Create the adjacent frame:

"The research desk for prediction markets — in your Telegram."
AI models audit every Kalshi market after each research run and publish their best picks with a public track record. See exactly how each model has performed, then copy any pick in one tap — paper money first, your own Kalshi account when you're ready.

Message hierarchy

PriorityMessageWhy this order
1. Lead"Every pick is public. So is every miss." The track record — hit rate, P&L, per-pick theses, settled outcomes anyone can audit.Trust is the scarce asset in a niche full of anonymous "signal groups." Radical transparency is the differentiator nobody can copy retroactively.
2. HookPodcast → market. "They said it on the pod. Here's the live market. Here's how that call aged."The most novel, demonstrable, shareable mechanic. Each instance is a self-contained piece of content. This is top-of-funnel, per the competitive research.
3. ComfortPaper first, your keys always. $100 simulated at live prices; live trading is your own Kalshi account, your own API key, never custody.Removes the two biggest objections (risk, custody) in one line. Post-"Polycule"-hack, non-custodial is the expected default — say it loudly.
4. PlumbingOne-tap copy, drift warnings, portfolio tracking, weekly digest.Table stakes. Must be flawless; never the headline.

What to stop saying

  • The current landing hero — "Your AI trades Kalshi while you feast" — promises autonomy you deliberately don't sell (auto-copy is insider-only and legally gray) and frames the product as a black-box bot rather than a research desk. Replace with the track-record frame above.
  • "Frontrun" as a public name. It connotes the one thing a CFTC-regulated venue's compliance team hates most. Ship as Kalmari everywhere; keep frontrun-bot as the repo name only.
  • Anything implying advice. House style: "the model's pick," "the audit found," "historical hit rate" — never "you should buy." Every public surface carries the hypothetical-performance footnote (audit §5, item 3).

Naming the proof

Give the research runs a recognizable, recurring product name — e.g. "The Audit" ("Tuesday's Audit found 7 mispriced markets") — so the content engine in §9 has a franchise to hang on. Models become characters with public records ("Opus is 12–4 on Fed markets"); the leaderboard already supports this framing natively.

4Pricing & packaging

Adopt the audit §7 recommendation as-is — it's correct and the current tiers are selling a thing (LLM token budgets) customers can no longer use. Summary, with GTM framing added:

TierPriceGetsGTM role
Free$0Paper trading, full leaderboard & /performance access, signals delayed 24–48h after each runThe funnel. Delayed picks let skeptics verify the record is real — the strongest converter — without giving away the live edge. Paper mode keeps them engaged indefinitely.
Pro$39/moReal-time picks + one-tap copy, live trading, weekly digest, 2 scheduled scansThe core offer. Anchor messaging to value, not cost: "one good pick at the historical hit rate pays for the month." $39 self-selects for funded Kalshi accounts.
Desk$99/moEverything + early delivery, follow-by-model/topic filters, API/webhook, unlimited schedulesThe "I trade seriously" tier. Also the future home of auto-copy if and when legal review clears it — until then it stays unsellable (TierInsider stays manual-grant).
  • Trial: 7 days, card-less, with real-time signals included — the trial must showcase the valuable thing, not the cheap thing. Add the ToS-acceptance step at activation (currently missing).
  • Annual = 2 months free, pitched after a user's first winning month (the digest knows when that is).
  • No performance fees, no profit share, no per-trade fee. The ~1% trade-fee norm from the bot ecosystem fits execution products; you sell research, and a flat subscription keeps you furthest from advice/brokerage characterization. Marginal cost per user is ~$0, so subscription margin is excellent.
  • Founding-member pricing at launch: first 100 Pro subscribers lock $29/mo for life. Creates urgency, rewards the community that seeds the track record, and caps the discount's cost.

5Launch preconditions

Marketing a money product with known integrity bugs is how you convert a launch into a reputation crater. The audit's P0 list is the gate; the items below are the subset that would specifically embarrass you publicly, restated as launch criteria:

  • Track-record integrity: fix SELL-signal scoring, the missing audit-source filter on /performance, and run_id dedup before driving any traffic to the page. One skeptic finding one wrong number on your proof page ends the trust pitch permanently. Add the hypothetical-performance footnote and switch bet sorting to chronological.
  • Live-mode safety: confirmation + key check on every path into live mode; clamp the 10-contract live default against real balance. The first "bot YOLOed my account" screenshot is unrecoverable.
  • Identity & access: key accounts on Telegram ID, independent cookie secret, separate whitelist from admin list, gate /test on an explicit dev flag, rotate the leaked tokens.
  • Crash-safe orders + automated DB backups. One VM disk currently holds the only copy of users' encrypted trading keys.
  • Pricing rework shipped (§4) — Stripe products, delayed-signals gating for Free, ToS acceptance.
  • Compliance pass: written read on Kalshi API/affiliate ToS and the advice boundary; geographic/age gates the ToS already promises; prominent non-custody + BYOK disclosure. Keep auto-copy unsold.
  • Replay-guard fix ("button expired — send /start") — the known "bot looks dead after deploy" issue is a first-session killer.

Everything else in the audit can be fixed behind the scenes post-launch. Don't let the long tail delay the date; let these specific items gate it.

6Launch sequence

Phase 0 — Private beta (now → ~4 weeks)

  • 20–30 hand-picked users from segment 1, personally onboarded. Goal: watch real funnels (start → first paper copy → live key → first live trade), fix what breaks, collect the first testimonials.
  • Run the audits on a fixed public cadence starting now — every week of pre-launch run history makes the launch-day track record stronger. The record should be 8–12 weeks deep before you ask strangers to trust it.
  • Ship the two highest-leverage conversion artifacts from the audit's ideas list: the "$100 per run since March → $X today" backtest widget on the landing page, and edge-remaining countdowns on signal cards.

Phase 1 — Community soft launch (weeks 5–8)

  • Open access, founding-member pricing live. Announce in 3–5 Kalshi-native communities (§7) as "a research experiment with a public record," not "a bot launch." The landing page leads with the performance page.
  • Begin the content engine (§9) on one channel (X) at full cadence.
  • Target: 100–300 users, ≥20 founding Pro subscribers, zero money-handling incidents.

Phase 2 — Public launch (weeks 9–14)

  • The launch artifact is the track record: "We published every AI pick for N weeks. Here's the full record — wins, losses, and the misses." That post/thread, plus a Product Hunt launch the same week, plus 2–3 podcast/newsletter placements (§7).
  • Turn on the referral loop and paper leagues (§8) the same week — launch traffic should land in mechanics that multiply it.

Phase 3 — Scale what worked (month 4+)

  • Double down on the 1–2 channels with the best activation-adjusted CAC; kill the rest. Consider small paid tests and the Hyperliquid/HIP-4 expansion question only now.

8Growth loops

Most of the machinery is already built — these are wiring jobs, not new systems:

  1. The proof loop (core). Research run → public picks → settled outcomes → auto-generated content → new users → more copy-trade data → richer track record. Runs entirely on existing data; needs only the auto-posting layer (§9).
  2. Share-a-pick. Signal share pages with OG images + /start sig_<id> deep links already exist. Add a referral parameter and a visible "shared by" credit so sharing is attributable and rewarded.
  3. Weekly recap cards. The digest already computes each user's week; share_image.go already renders OG images. Generate "My week: +12% · 5W–2L" cards with a referral link, offered in every digest. Verified-record flexing is the native marketing behavior of trading products.
  4. Paper leagues in group chats. Bot joins a Telegram group → weekly paper-trading league, shared leaderboard, trophy share-image for the winner. Group-native is Telegram's structural advantage and the onboarding is already paper-safe. Every league is a multi-person acquisition event.
  5. Referral program. Give-a-month/get-a-month on Pro. The proven Telegram-bot growth mechanic (Trojan/Banana Gun) adapted to subscription economics. Keep it simple; no tokenomics.
  6. Outcome notifications as retention. When a copied market settles, message the result with the user's running record ("That's 4 of your last 5 ✅"). Settlement detection already runs hourly; the emotional payoff of wins is currently delivered to nobody. Retention is a growth loop — churned users don't refer.

9The content engine

The product generates its own content; the job is packaging, not writing. Build a small render-and-post pipeline off the existing public APIs (/api/leaderboard, /api/performance, signals data):

CadenceArtifactSource

Every settlementResult card: thesis excerpt, entry, outcome, P&L — posted on wins and losses alike. The losses are what make the wins credible.Settlement jobs
WeeklyPerformance thread: equity curve, hit rate, best/worst call with the model's reasoning, model-vs-model standings./performance data
Per podcast episode"5 claims → 5 markets" + speaker running records.Podcast pipeline
MonthlyMethodology post: what the models got wrong and what changed. Builds the researcher identity; this is also where the v2 work (calibration, CLV, clusters) becomes marketing material once it graduates.v2 analytics

Editorial rules: never delete a miss; never reorder to flatter (chronological, per the audit); every public number must reconcile with the performance page; hypothetical-performance disclaimer on anything resembling a return figure. The content engine is only as valuable as its credibility, and credibility is single-use.

12The 90-day plan

Weeks 1–2 — Foundation

  • Fix launch-precondition list (§5): track-record integrity, live-mode safety, identity/access, backups.
  • Ship pricing rework (§4) + ToS acceptance; rename public surfaces to Kalmari; rewrite landing hero to the track-record frame.
  • Lock the audit cadence; start the public clock on the track record.

Weeks 3–4 — Private beta

  • Onboard 20–30 segment-1 users personally; instrument the funnel (§10).
  • Ship the backtest widget and edge-remaining countdowns; fix what beta breaks.
  • Stand up the X account; begin settled-pick posting (manually at first is fine).

Weeks 5–8 — Soft launch

  • Open access + founding-member pricing; announce in 3–5 Kalshi communities.
  • Content engine to full cadence on X; public Telegram broadcast channel live with delayed picks.
  • Ship outcome notifications and weekly recap share cards.
  • Gate to proceed: ≥100 users, ≥40% activation, ≥20 founding Pros, zero money incidents.

Weeks 9–12 — Public launch

  • The track-record launch thread + Product Hunt + 2–3 newsletter/podcast placements in the same week.
  • Referral program and paper leagues live; podcast-claim content at per-episode cadence.
  • First monthly methodology post (including anything that went wrong — establish the honesty norm early).

Week 13 — Review and decide

  • Channel review: keep the top 2 by activation-adjusted acquisition, cut the rest.
  • Decide the phase-3 questions with data: paid tests? Hyperliquid/HIP-4 second venue? Desk-tier feature investment (follow-by-model, API)? Auto-copy legal review?
