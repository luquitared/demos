Motivation

Egocentric human-demonstration sample for robot-manipulation foundation-model training, assembled for the Google DeepMind Human Ego Data pilot. Collected and produced by Synjuku.

Composition

  • 600 episodes × 60 s = 10.0 h, 4 synchronized cameras per episode (head/ego, left wrist, right wrist, exocentric), 960×720 @ 29.97 fps, H.264. Video 59.3 GB (dataset ~61 GB total).
  • 9,384 language annotations (subtask spans, median 3.2 s, one sentence each), 100% machine-verified; label coverage averages 86% of each clip — unverifiable or incorrect labels were removed rather than shipped.
  • Per-frame tracks, merged into each episode's data parquet (21 columns/frame): image-space hand pose (21 keypoints/hand), ego camera position + rotation (COLMAP SLAM, up-to-scale), exo upper-body pose (9 joints), left/right hand pose in 3D world coordinates, with per-track timestamps and validity flags (camera position & upper-body tracks).

Quality control

  • Signal layer: 2,400/2,400 streams checked (resolution, fps, duration, black/freeze, SHA-256 integrity).
  • Label layer: every annotation verified against its own footage; labels that could not be verified were corrected or removed. Details and measured results in the QA section below.
  • Tracking quality (mean detection): camera pose 96.2% · body 98.4% · L hand 95.4% · R hand 93.2%; medians ≈99.3%. Per-episode rates in meta/tracking_quality_per_episode.csv.
  • Versioned: annotation states snapshotted v1.0→v1.4 with every label change logged.

Access & use

Read-only scoped S3 credentials to s3://synjuku-data/synjuku-gdmr-sample/. LeRobot-v3-compatible layout; tracks are pre-merged per frame — no joins required. Intended uses: manipulation policy/representation learning, hand-trajectory prediction, subtask grounding. Showcase renders (wrist-trajectory overlay, 3D pose) are included under viz/. License per Synjuku–GDM pilot terms. Maintained by the Synjuku data team; per-episode provenance enables regeneration of any artifact.

How labels were verified

  • Every label checked against its footage. Each subtask span was individually verified against the video segment it describes by an automated video–language verification system, calibrated on a 240-span human-annotated reference set.
  • Corrected or removed — never shipped wrong. Labels that failed verification were re-captioned from the footage and re-verified before inclusion; labels that could not be confirmed were removed. Corrected labels are provenance-marked in the annotation files, so original and corrected labels are always distinguishable.
  • Independently audited. A stratified re-sample of the final annotations (3 spans × 600 episodes) returned 0 contradictions in 1,800 spans. Because automated verification has known limits on subtle action distinctions, we state estimated label accuracy as 90–95% rather than claiming the audit's 100%.
